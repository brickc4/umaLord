umaLord - Uma Musume: Pretty Derby (Global) account helper
==========================================================

A Windows app that holds your roster and the full Global game data from uma.guide. It
also has a career planner, a built-in race simulator, a results log, and an "Ask" tab
where Claude answers questions about your account.


Run it
------
1. Unzip the whole folder anywhere. Keep the other folders next to umalord.exe.
2. Double-click umalord.exe.
   The app isn't signed, so Windows may say "Windows protected your PC". Click
   "More info", then "Run anyway".

Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.


First run
---------
The app starts empty and opens Setup, which offers two ways in:
- Load a profile: if someone saved your account for you, you'll have a file ending in
  .umalord.json. Put it in the profiles folder next to umalord.exe, leave it in Downloads,
  or drag it onto the app's window, then click Choose and Load this profile.
- Set up from scratch: pick a trainer name and how the app looks, then click every uma and
  support card you own (they light up).
  - Set "New picks start as" first: for example MLB, then click every maxed card, then LB0
    for the rest. Each card is one click.
  - Hover an uma you added to click her stars and potential right on the picture; hover a
    card to click its limit break diamonds.
  - Ctrl-click or Shift-click picks several at once, to set them all together.
  - "Paste a list" takes lines like "Kitasan Black MLB" or "Oguri Cap Starlight Beat 5* P5".
  - Keys: type to search (Enter adds the first match), arrows to move, Space to add or
    remove, 1-5 for stars (Shift for potential) or 0-4 for a card's limit break, Ctrl+Z to
    undo.
Settings (the cog, top right) > Profile can rename the account, save your data to one file (Save my data), load
another profile, reopen Setup, or empty the app (Start over). Loading a profile or starting
over saves what was there to profiles\backups first, so nothing is lost.


Tabs
----
New to the app? Press the ? button (top right) for Help: it explains every tab,
the steps to plan a career, and the game words the app uses.

- Home: where to start each day.
  - The next Champions Meeting: announced or estimated, its course drawn with corners and
    slopes, countdowns in your time zone, the skills it turns off, what the community meta
    sheet recommends, your best umas for it, and how far your plan for it has got.
    "Plan for it" starts a plan with its course filled in; "Continue plan" opens yours.
  - Coming up: the next three weeks from the game's news. Click a bar to read it.
  - Widgets: where you left off, your stable, cards by type, who can run where, an uma of
    the day, your trainer card, your best and missing cards for the next CM, and a game
    term a day. Two per row on a wide window, one per row on a narrow one.
- Collection: everything you own, in three sub-tabs.
  - Filters are on the left (the << button folds them away). Quick views set common
    filters in one click, and "Save these filters as a view" keeps your own. The filters
    that are on show above the results: click one to remove it, or Reset all.
  - Sort with the box above the results, or click a table column's header: both agree.
    S / M / L changes the picture size. Table shows more columns (Columns picks which;
    Copy as CSV copies them for a spreadsheet).
  - Umas show aptitudes, stats and growth, skills (with what is locked at your potential),
    evolutions, career objectives and events. The aptitude grid on the left keeps umas
    graded A or better (click again: S only) at a surface, distance or style.
  - Support cards show support effects at your level next to MLB, the unique effect,
    hints and events. Filter by limit break, by a skill a card teaches, or by an effect's
    minimum (for example Friendship 25 or more). Hover a card for its numbers, to put it
    in your plan's deck, or to compare it with another card side by side.
  - "+ Add cards" (Support cards) shows every card's picture. Click one to set its limit
    break and level, or click a diamond on it to save it at that LB at once. It saves
    account\support_cards.md and downloads any missing pictures.
  - Skills: every Global skill, what it does, and where you can get it. "Ones I can get"
    shows only skills from umas and cards you own. Pick a Champions Meeting or a course
    on the left to see only skills that can trigger there; "Test on a course" races an
    uma with and without the skill.
- Career: plan a career, race it, and log what happened, in three sub-tabs.
- Career > Plan: five steps down the page. Pick a goal, the training scenario and a
  trainee, and build a deck by clicking a slot and choosing from the card art.
  - The scenario (URA Finale, Unity Cup, Trackblazer, Grand Concert) is picked for you
    from the next Champions Meeting. It decides training values, skill-point sources and
    which cards are scenario-linked.
  - The ✨ buttons fill things in for you: the best-fitting trainees, a whole deck or just
    the empty slots, and the skills to buy.
  - Meta: for a Champions Meeting, the "Meta" card shows what the community PvP sheet
    (Lightning, Daptrius and Henry's "PvP Deck & Uma Recommendation" Google Sheet)
    recommends: the styles that do best, target stats, trainees, decks for beginners,
    regular players and whales, and skills. The ✨ buttons pick its cards, trainees and
    skills first (marked ★), and the simulations choose the rest. A card you have at a low
    limit break counts for less.
  - Cards are scored by simulating careers in the plan's scenario with the rest of the
    deck (stats toward your target stats, skill points, scenario link) plus the skills
    they teach, valued on the target course for the trainee's style. Hover a card to see
    why. They are suggestions from a model; knowledge\scenarios.json lists its
    assumptions.
  - Skills to buy: "Auto-pick" chooses skills within the skill points the career simulation
    expects you to end with (or a budget you set). It picks at once, then races the skills
    to find the set that gains the most together (about 10-20 seconds): each step adds the
    skill whose measured gain on top of those already chosen is best for its cost, then it
    tries swaps and checks the set in 1000 races. The table shows each skill's gain alone,
    its gain in the set, how often it triggered, and why it was or wasn't bought;
    "Explain" lists every step. Gains don't add up: skills overlap.
  - Pin a skill (the dot left of its name: pin, then ban) to always or never buy it.
    Debuffs and skills that depend on where other umas are can't be valued by the
    simulator, so they're listed under "Not simulated": pin the ones you want.
  - "Meta sheet" sets how much the community meta sheet counts: Off, Tie-break (the
    default) or Strong (its skills first). "Fast Learner" takes 10% off every skill.
  - At the end of a real career, tick "I'm at the skill shop now": enter your final stats
    and SP, tick the skills on offer with their hint levels (or paste the list), and
    Auto-pick works on exactly those.
  - Step 2 warns when the trainee's grade for the goal is below B, and what it costs her.
  - "Simulate" races the plan on the Race sim page: the trainee with her skills against
    the same build without them. "Save to plan" there writes the results into the plan.
  - "Open Umalator" and "Copy for Umalator" still send the build to https://umalator.app/
    if you want the website.
  - "Save plan" writes it to the plans folder.
- Career > Race sim: the Moomoolator race simulator (umalator.app), built into the app.
  No browser needed.
  - Race setup (left): pick a Champions Meeting to load its course and conditions, or any
    course yourself. "What to test": Compare, Skills (how many lengths each skill adds),
    Stamina (HP left at the finish) or Quick (an instant stamina estimate). The rarely
    needed options are under "Advanced options".
  - The two builds sit side by side. "Use meta stats" fills the meta sheet's stats for
    the CM; "Stat templates" has other starting points; Swap and Copy move builds around.
    "Load from Planner" loads the plan's build.
  - Compare's results start with the verdict (who wins, how often, by how much, and
    whether that's more than noise), then five tabs:
    - Overview: the gap of every race as a chart (or the cumulative share), and where
      along the course the lengths are won.
    - Race: the course, Uma 2's lead along it, speed and HP, all lined up. Replay plays
      the typical race with two dots and skills popping up.
    - Skills: where each skill fired and how often (grey rows never fired, with why),
      and two buttons: what each of Uma 2's skills is worth, and where her next training
      points matter most. They run in the background.
    - Stamina: HP left at the finish, and what a full spurt needs.
    - Runs: your last 10 runs (pin one to compare against), each uma's numbers, and
      Copy summary / Save chart as PNG / Save to plan.
- Career > Results: log careers, Team Trials, Champions Meeting, League of Heroes and
  sparks.
- Lineage: your next career, and why. It opens on an Overview of where each plan stands,
  with what you did lately (Undo takes a "marked done" back). Nothing simulates until you
  press Create plan, Re-plan or Reset; making a plan shows each stage as it runs, with
  Cancel, and the plan keeps that log under "How this plan was chosen". A saved plan has
  Mark done (pick the finished uma that ran the career), Edit (keep or leave out runners;
  the changes wait until you re-plan), Re-plan, and Reset in the "..." menu. When you
  import finished umas or the meta sheet is read again, the plan says so and you choose.
  A plan shows what it's for, then every career in order (who, why, how many tries it
  usually takes); "Show how" opens a step's details. "Your team, before and after" shows
  each race's best finished umas now beside the team after the plan. Point at an
  underlined word to see what it means.
  Three views:
  - Champions Meeting: three runners for the next CM, each against what you can field
    today (your finished umas as they are, raced in the simulator), and the parent careers
    they need.
  - Team Trials: the five races, with today's runners, the plan and the gain for each.
  - One runner: one runner's parent plan, step by step. Past the point where 9 in 10
    players would have finished a step, the Stuck panel offers to settle with your closest
    uma, try another route, or keep going. Each choice is previewed first, and Undo takes
    it back.
  Each view starts with the Next career card: who to train and why, the parents, scenario,
  deck and skills, and how to run it. Parent careers use Independent Training: copy the
  setup panel into the game and tick the skills as you enter them (the ticks are saved).
  Runners are manual careers. A runner's card lists her races: her objectives, then G1s that fit her for fans. At the end of her career, "Open the skill shop helper" opens Career > Plan with her plan in skill shop mode: enter her final stats, SP and the skills on offer, and it picks the best set. On a wide window
  the card sits beside the overview, and each side scrolls on its own.
- Notes and files: read the notes, plans and logs. It isn't a tab any more: open it from
  Settings > Data and updates ("Browse notes and data files").
- Long dropdowns (trainees, courses, skills, models) have a search box: type to filter,
  and delete the text to see the whole list again.
- Ask: ask questions about your account in plain English, or ask it to do things: "Plan
  the next Champions Meeting for my best Pace uma and simulate it." It builds the plan
  (trainee, deck, skills) in the Planner, races it in the Race sim, and shows each step in
  the chat as a card with "Open in Planner" or "Open in Race sim" and an Undo button.
  - It changes the plan and the Race sim at once (Undo puts them back). Saving a plan,
    adding a result or changing your roster shows a card with Approve and Deny first.
    Settings > AI assistant can let it save plans and log results without asking; roster
    changes always ask.
  - It runs at most four simulations a question unless you ask for more. ■ Stop ends the
    answer and any simulation it started.
  There are two ways to answer:
  - OpenCode, the default, is free, with no key and no account needed.
    - It uses OpenCode (opencode.ai), which runs one of OpenCode's free AI models to read
      your roster and the game data.
    - The first time you open Ask, click "Download OpenCode" (about 59 MB). The app fetches
      the official release, checks it against its published checksum, and installs it in
      tools\opencode.
    - It can read your files but can't edit them or run commands; it works only through
      umaLord's own tools, and file changes go through the Approve cards.
    - Free models are only offered for a limited time and are weaker than Claude. Pick one
      in Settings > AI assistant. The note under the model says whether your questions are
      kept.
  - Claude gives better answers. With Claude Code installed (claude.com/claude-code), it can
    use your Claude account: either sign Claude Code in (run `claude`, then /login) and leave
    the key box empty, or run `claude setup-token` and paste the sk-ant-oat01-... it prints
    into Settings > AI assistant. Use counts against your Claude plan. Or paste an Anthropic
    API key from https://console.anthropic.com (works without Claude Code too), billed per use,
    or set the ANTHROPIC_API_KEY environment variable.


Game knowledge: the Help window's word list and Home's "Did you know?" card come from
knowledge\mechanics.md, notes gathered from community guides (GameTora, uma.guide and
community sheets), each with its source. knowledge\racecourses.md describes every Champions
Meeting course (corners, slopes, how much Stamina each style needs), and
knowledge\skills.md gives skill advice, including a community tier list that the Skills page
and the planner's "Not simulated" list show as "Tier list: CM ... TT ...".

Settings (the cog at the top right, or Ctrl+comma) has five sections. Everything saves as
you change it.
- Profile: your trainer name, the data folder, and saving, loading or emptying the account.
- Appearance: the mode (System follows Windows, or always Light or Dark), six colour themes
  (Seiun Sky, Sakura, Sunset, Starry night, Track day and the high-contrast Ink), the sky
  and decorations behind the pages, where chibi Seiun Sky appears (everywhere, small beside
  each page title; on Home and empty pages only; or nowhere: she never covers your data),
  the size of everything, and comfortable or compact spacing.
- Data and updates: how old the game data, the meta sheet and the skill tier list are, and
  how to refresh them.
- AI assistant: who answers on the Ask tab, the model, your API key, and what the assistant
  may do without asking.
- About: the version, credits, license and keyboard shortcuts.

Messages such as "Saved" pop up at the bottom of the window for a few seconds.

On a small window (down to 960x600) the side panels make room: the Collection filters fold
into a strip of icons while a table is shown (click >> to open them again), the planner's
saved plans become a + button and a folder menu, and the status bar at the bottom shortens
its labels (hover one for the full text).

Keyboard shortcuts (also listed in Help):
- Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+5: Home, Collection, Career, Lineage, Ask
- Ctrl+F: jump to the page's search box
- Ctrl+S: save the plan (on Career > Plan)
- F5: reload your roster, game data and plans
- Esc: close Help, a dialog, or the details panel
- ?: open Help
- +: you pulled another copy of the card or uma whose details are open: one more LB or star


Your roster
-----------
account\umas.md and account\support_cards.md hold the roster. Setup and the "+ Add umas" /
"+ Add cards" buttons in Collection edit them for you. Pulled another copy of something you
own? Open it in Collection and click the small + beside its LB diamonds or stars; it saves at
once, and the message that pops up has Undo. You can also edit the files in any text
editor: keep the table columns as they are, and press Reload in the app.
Each row's ID is the uma.guide ID of that uma or card.

Your finished umas (the parents you can use) come from the game itself. On Windows, run
UmaExtractor (github.com/xancia/UmaExtractor) with the game open on Enhance > Veteran List;
on Linux, run `sudo python3 -I tools/read_veterans.py -o <your downloads folder>/data.json`
(root's Python needs the msgpack package; with Nix, run sudo on a Python built with it).
Then Settings > Profile > Import finished umas (or drop data.json on the window). The app
lists each one in account\parents.md with its sparks, its parents and its G1 wins, and adds
any uma you've trained but hadn't listed, or raises her stars or potential to what you trained
her at. Each import replaces the last (the game's list is the truth); the previous one is kept
in profiles\backups.
Both tools only read the game's memory; reading another program's memory may be against the
game's terms, so use them at your own judgment.


Updating the game data
----------------------
The Champions Meeting schedule updates by itself: when the app starts (at most every 6
hours) it reads the game's official news on umamusume.com and Umalator's presets, in the
background. Announced events show their league selection, Round 1 and end times in your
time zone, and the skills that won't activate (auto-pick never buys those). Events not
announced yet are marked "Estimated". Turn this off, or refresh by hand, in Settings > Data
and updates. Without internet the app uses what it fetched last time, or the data it shipped
with.

knowledge\data was built from uma.guide on 2026-10-16, and knowledge\data\sim (the race
simulator's skills and courses) from umalator.app on 2026-10-17. To refresh them, install
Python 3, then run this from the umaLord folder:

    python -I -X utf8 tools\update_knowledge.py

To refresh only the community meta sheet (it changes most often), add "--only meta".

The game data also refreshes itself each week: a data pack, built and tested from uma.guide,
brings new umas, cards and their pictures. Only the files that changed download. Check it
or run it now in Settings > Data and updates > Weekly pack.


Using it with Claude Code (optional)
------------------------------------
Open this folder in Claude Code. CLAUDE.md tells Claude how to:
- keep the roster up to date from screenshots,
- run simulations for saved plans,
- log results, and keep its opinions in insights.md.


Rebuilding the app (optional)
-----------------------------
The source is in the app folder. You need Rust (rustup) with the
stable-x86_64-pc-windows-gnu toolchain and its llvm-tools component.
Run app\setup-toolchain.ps1 once, then run "cargo build --release" in the app folder.

Troubleshooting
---------------
- The window is blank or closes at once: the app draws with OpenGL, so update the
  graphics driver.
- The status bar says "Knowledge missing": the knowledge\data folder is gone. Unzip the
  whole folder again.
